Method and sources

Play to Grow method and sources

Play to Grow is currently a testable working hypothesis, not an evidence-based treatment. Observable actions are kept separate from clinical diagnosis.

In briefChoice, concrete action, agreed rules, and progress review.

Working hypothesis

When gaming is already a strong source of motivation, it may be connected to small real-life actions chosen by the child from a suitable set. A clear report-and-reward cycle may help a family discuss effort in concrete terms instead of through a general conflict about games.

Pilot principles

  • Child choice within safe options.
  • Small observable quests instead of abstract demands.
  • Rules and rewards agreed before completion.
  • A parent confirms both the result and actual reward delivery.
  • A psychologist considers context, progress, and unintended effects.
  • The plan changes if motivation becomes pressure or increases conflict.

What is observed

The pilot may track quest completion, continued participation, independent choice, the quality of family agreements, and subjective reports of conflict. These measures are not a diagnosis and do not demonstrate treatment effectiveness.

Official sources

WHO describes gaming disorder through impaired control, priority given to gaming over other activities, and continuation despite negative consequences when this causes significant functional impairment. Diagnosis belongs to a qualified professional; a high amount of gaming time alone is not sufficient.

  • WHO: Gaming disorder in ICD-11
  • WHO: Q&A on gaming disorder
  • American Academy of Pediatrics: Family Media Plan
